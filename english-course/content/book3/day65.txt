@diff: 开头先说目的——I'm writing to…
@d_cn: 给客户写邮件：先问候、介绍自己、交代背景，最后才说「所以想请您……」。
@d_en: Hi Daniel, I'm writing to confirm the new delivery times. / I'm writing to ask about… / I'm writing to let you know that…
@d_why: 中文书信讲究「先礼后事」：先寒暄、再铺垫背景，最后才提出请求，一上来就说事会显得唐突。美国职场的邮件正好反过来：对方一天收几十上百封邮件，打开后多半只看**发件人、主题行和第一句话**，所以**第一句就要说目的**——这正是张涛「先说结论」的意思。I'm writing to + 动词原形 是最稳妥的开头，动词本身就点明了目的：confirm（确认）、ask about（询问）、let you know（告知）。这不是不礼貌：在英文里，**节省对方的时间本身就是礼貌**。寒暄最多一句（Hope you're doing well.），也可以完全不写；对方早就认识你，就别再自我介绍了。林晓草稿的开头 I hope this email finds you well 很常见，但不少美国人觉得它是套话；对一天收两百封邮件的 Daniel，它只会把真正的问题挤到后面去。

@output_sample: Leo, here's the most important rule for work emails: put your main point first. Our sellers get hundreds of emails a day, so they often read only the subject line and the first sentence. That first sentence should be a summary of the whole email. Start with "I'm writing to" and a verb, for example, "I'm writing to confirm the new delivery times." Don't write three lines of greetings, and don't introduce yourself again. Next, put the details below in bullet points. Short lines are easier to read, especially with numbers. If something might be confusing, add one sentence with "To clarify." Then ask for one clear action, and ask politely. Don't write "Reply today." It sounds like an order. Write "Could you please reply at your earliest convenience?" or give a date, like "by Friday, if possible." Finally, keep the ending short. One sentence is enough, for example, "Let me know if you have any questions." Then write "Best regards" and your name. This morning, my first draft to Daniel had five paragraphs, and Tom sent it back. My new version has a few short lines, and Tom read it in ten seconds. Before you click Send, read your email again and check the tone.
